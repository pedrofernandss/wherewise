Model

v1 uses rule-based aquifer zones, an illustrative water-table regression and interpolated station rainfall. v2 replaces them with real data: BGS aquifer productivity (borehole yield and aquifer favourability), GLOBGM water-table depth, CHIRPS rainfall and SoilGrids soil texture. The Validation panel compares both against real water points (WPdx).

Horizon

Irrigation project

A hypothetical irrigation scheme evaluated in every cell: is a borehole or river pumping the better way to supply it?

The area you want to irrigate. It sets the water demand: area × 5 mm/day pumped in 8 h/day (5 ha ≈ 8.7 L/s). More area means more boreholes (limited by the aquifer's yield, max. 20) and a stricter test of whether 10% of the river flow can cover it.

Price of pumping energy (default: off-grid diesel/solar equivalent). It multiplies the operating cost of both options, so the higher it is, the more the water lift weighs: water-table depth for boreholes, river-to-field height and pipe length for rivers.

Deepest water table you consider viable. Borehole success probability is multiplied by (1 − depth ÷ WTD_max): a water table at half this depth halves it, and beyond it the borehole is ruled out.

Farthest a river intake is considered. River suitability falls linearly with distance and reaches zero at this value; rivers farther away are discarded. Raising it opens more cells to river pumping.
